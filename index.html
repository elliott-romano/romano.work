Bio

Elliott [b. 1989] is a graphic designer/educator specializing in product, motion, & interactive design projects. His studio and research practice investigates the design of software and expressive tools that make people better at what they do - or reveal something totally new, often in real-time. He holds an masters degree from Rhode Island School and based in the pacific northwest.

Instrument MFA Thesis (2025)

Print, Research

Instrument is the culmination of a three-year inquiry into what it means to design tools that perform. It is part research project and part artist book. The thesis explores how real-time feedback systems, both analog and digital, can function as graphic instruments: systems that generate visual and linguistic output through performance. Through essays, interviews, and custom tools of my own design, the book frames design as an instrumental practice that exists in the space between recording and live expression. Docker Tag Mutability (2025)

Product

Working with the Docker Product Team I designed and helped implement iterative updates to workflows in managing images Docker Hub, the primary container registry used by developers and organizations worldwide. The feature introduced tag immutability controls, giving teams precise control over how image tags are updated and preventing accidental overwrites that could disrupt builds or deployments.

Chladni Sans (2024)

Identity

Chladni Sans is a video-based typeface inspired by the cymatic patterns created by Chladni Plates. Each letterform was recorded in sand on a vibrating plate, creating a unique and dynamic transformations of the letterforms as the plate vibrates. The intent of this typeface was to create an expressive tool for visually representing sound. The typeface explores the intersection of sound, vibration, and visual form, creating a unique typographic system that responds to audio input and generates dynamic letterforms.

DigitalOcean Product Onboarding (2024)

Product

Designed and implemented a comprehensive onboarding experience for DigitalOcean's product suite, helping new users understand and navigate the platform's core services. The onboarding flow guides users through key features while maintaining engagement and reducing time-to-value. Various Flora (2025)

Motion, Research

Various Flora was a public exhibition at the RISD Museum in Providence, RI. The exhibition featured a collection of Machine-Learning generated flora trained from the museum's vast collection of botanical prints. Each piece explored the intersection of traditional botanical illustration and contemporary AI-generated imagery, creating hybrid forms that question the boundaries between natural and artificial representation. The exhibition included interactive installations, printed works, and digital displays that demonstrated the potential of AI as a creative tool in botanical art and scientific visualization.

Cellular Autonomies (2024)

Print

Cellular Autonomies is a study into natural systems as a means of generating improvised procedural form. Using custom software based on Stephen Wolfram's original Cellular Autonoma algorithms as a means of generative pattern making, a series of monoprints further abstract the gesture of unpredicatble growth from one element to the next. The collection exists as a series of large scale monoprints and limited edition risograph booklets. The project explores the intersection of computational algorithms and traditional printmaking techniques, creating unique visual patterns that emerge from simple rules and complex interactions.
